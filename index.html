Sources and calculation

The WOW Impact Engine reports monthly litres and total distributions of R33,556 over 17,135 litres from October 2025 to January 2026. Monthly figures add to 17,134 litres. The projected rebate per litre uses the reported period totals at full precision. The starting vehicle count and baseline turns are assumptions.

Annual fuel volume starts at 12 times the selected month’s litres. Scale by participating vehicles divided by starting vehicles, then by target turns divided by baseline turns. Turning off additional demand caps fuel per vehicle at the starting annual rate. Each turn represents a funded cycle with constant litres per vehicle per turn.

The selected WOW scenario supplies the fuel price, merchant settlement rate, rebate funding rate and recipient shares. Cash-in must settle for purchases to proceed. Reported litres replace the example’s batch size. Each annual projection rounds cash flows to cents.

    How to verify social impact

    The WOW Impact Engine defines value returned by beneficiary class. Here the cash flows are scenarios. Verified results require matching sponsor settlement to wallet credits, reversals and the member registry for the reporting period.

    Count each active member once across turns. Use consented registry data to report women’s and youth participation and their shares of funded value. To estimate changes in household income, travel costs or safety, compare baseline and follow-up surveys and publish dates and sample sizes.

    The comparison estimates additional sales. A pilot can test how much trade would have happened through cash, another lender or another merchant. Profit and local value added require production-cost data. IFC guidance on counterfactuals

    Research behind the estimates
    • IFC found payment terms of 30–90 days between MSME suppliers and anchor firms in a representative Kenyan supply chain. We use 30 days as an illustrative starting point for the cash cycle. IFC Kenya supply-chain assessment (2022)
    • HSBC and ING completed a soybean trade’s letter-of-credit document exchange in 24 hours. Paper exchanges usually took 5–10 days. The scenarios remove 0, 2, 5 or 9 days from a whole cycle only if that work delays cash collection. Production time and payment terms can limit the gain. HSBC and ING transaction (2018)
    • In a Niger field experiment, mobile delivery cut variable cash-transfer costs by about 20%, while initial costs were higher. Recipients waited about 30 minutes on average, compared with four hours for cash. We test cost reductions of 0%, 10%, 20% and 30% of addressable processing costs. Customer price savings depend on the assumed pass-through. Aker et al., Niger cash-transfer experiment (2016)
    • For a customer-price benchmark, the World Bank found average charges of 4.59% for digital services and 7.30% for non-digital services when sending US$200 abroad in Q3 2025. This cross-border comparison gives context only. Merchant savings in the calculator use the editable local cost base. World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide, issue 54

    The 5% cost base and the split between customers (50%), investors (25%) and sponsors (25%) are illustrative commercial assumptions. FORUS pilot data or signed terms can replace them.
