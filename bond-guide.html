1. Capital enters the issuer

The proposal assumes that the entire capital stack is funded at inception. Senior and junior notes carry simple annual coupons and a year-five principal bullet. Unpaid coupons carry forward without compounding. Catalytic capital has no fixed coupon and receives only cash remaining after the note claims.

The use-of-proceeds budget is not an additional payment on top of annual advances. The workbook deploys USD 60 million in year one, USD 80 million in year two, then up to the available USD 85 million in later base-case years.

2. The operating flow generates collections

  1. Approve and fund productive activity. Verify the cooperative, members, crop plan, input budget, suppliers and buyer terms. Release approved advances through the nominated financial provider. Undeployed principal remains cash.
  2. Record goods and buyer acceptance. Link each financed lot to custody, weight, grade, accepted quantity and the buyer invoice. Delivery and invoicing establish evidence of a sale; the bank credit establishes collected cash.
  3. Allocate the cleared buyer receipt. Pay the contractually protected farmer proceeds and other agreed exclusions first. Apply the agreed financing sweep to eligible receipts, subject to the remaining repayment cap. The cooperative keeps the unswept amount.
  4. Route the financing share to the issuer. Only the contracted financing allocation enters the issuer waterfall. Gross crop sales and local farmer proceeds are not added to bond debt-service cash.
  5. Reconcile principal, premium and delay. The annual model recovers the advance principal first, then treats the excess as financing income. A one-year delay creates a receivable, not cash available for distribution.

3. A cleared sale: USD 1,900

The annex’s example accepts 95 sacks of 50 kg, or 4.75 tonnes, at USD 400 per tonne. Five further sacks remain on quality hold and create no eligible receipt in this example. The buyer’s cleared payment is USD 1,900. With USD 400 protected and a 20% sweep, the issuer receives USD 300, assuming at least USD 300 remains under the facility’s cap.

Receipt reconciliation: USD 400 protected + USD 1,200 unswept + USD 300 financing = USD 1,900 cleared cash. Local proceeds total USD 1,600. The remaining financing cap falls by USD 300.

5. The trustee pays the investor waterfall

  1. Priority operating costs and taxes. Pay the modelled servicing and operating allowance, including carried unpaid priority costs. The source model can consume principal cash when financing income is insufficient.
  2. Senior interest, then scheduled senior principal. Include unpaid senior coupons. Before maturity the ring-fenced reserve can support senior coupons only. Principal is scheduled at maturity.
  3. Junior interest, then scheduled junior principal. Include unpaid junior coupons. There is no assumed reserve support for junior coupons before maturity.
  4. Before maturity, restore reserve and retain surplus. Refill the reserve to its target using remaining distributable cash. Retain any excess. Neither reserve nor retained surplus is assumed to fund new advances.
  5. At maturity, release cash and absorb losses in order. Available principal, reserve and retained surplus enter the final waterfall. After priority costs and note claims, return catalytic capital up to its original contribution. Any remaining residual follows the signed beneficiary terms.

New advances stop after a prior year closes with senior coupon arrears or unpaid priority expenses. Junior coupon arrears carry forward but do not by themselves trigger this stop in the supplied model.

The base case generates USD 5.40 million of first-year operating income after costs against USD 6.90 million of current coupons: 0.78× operating coupon cover. It pays the senior coupon and USD 0.50 million of the junior coupon in year one. Junior coupon arrears clear in year four. Year-five cash before investor distributions is USD 102.95 million, including USD 10 million released from reserve and USD 0.30 million of previously trapped surplus.

Base-case catalytic investors recover USD 6.05 million of their USD 10 million contribution, leaving a USD 3.95 million capital loss. The cause is the USD 5 million setup spend, partly offset by USD 1.05 million of cumulative financing surplus after operating costs and note coupons: USD 10 million − USD 5 million + USD 1.05 million = USD 6.05 million returned. The base case recovers all deployed advance principal. Delayed junior coupons give an annual investor IRR of approximately 9.69%, below the stated 10% simple coupon.

The delayed and severe cases exhaust the opening reserve, develop senior arrears and stop new advances from year four. Reserve support changes timing; it does not eliminate loss. The simulator exposes unpaid coupon claims separately from unpaid principal and unrecovered catalytic capital.

7. Where the FORUS platform fits

Evidence and workflow

FORUS Register links organisations, members and their mandates. Grow records crop plans, budgets and draw conditions. Merchants records dealers, orders, deliveries and settlement details. Trade links custody, grade, accepted quantity, invoices and buyer payments. IOBOXX coordinates approvals and exceptions.

Execution and distribution

Rail Connector and the licensed provider execute authorised instructions. Bank confirmation establishes cleared fiat cash. The independent trustee applies the agreed payment priorities. The financing can use ordinary bank money and a conventional noteholder register.

Tokenised notes, settlement instruments, agricultural entitlements and member rewards carry different rights. MHX issuance, token market prices, voucher face value and programme votes contribute zero debt-service cash in this model. The first pilot country remains undecided.

8. Assumptions that determine the result

Portfolio settings cover programme size, capital shares, coupons, reserve and setup, deployment targets, eligible sales per advance, revenue sweep, repayment cap, incremental noncollection, servicing costs, FX haircut and one-year collection delay. Change the selected case to load the source assumptions, then adjust inputs and apply them to recalculate.

The source workbook simplifies productive assets into annual financing cohorts and assumes their principal can be recovered on that schedule. Storage and processing assets would require a detailed construction, operating and repayment schedule before pricing an actual issue. The model assumes no new borrowing, refinancing, automatic reserve replenishment, insurance recoveries or gains on idle cash. Collections due after year five are excluded from maturity cash and shown separately when present.

The simulator calculates transfers in cents. Small differences can arise when individual transfers are rounded to cents rather than calculated in unrounded USD millions as in the workbook. It applies the stated expense-first priority to cash released at maturity if custom assumptions leave priority expenses unpaid; this edge case is identified in the scenario warning.
